## A. The combined prompt (P11: all thirteen, combined)

> Use the Maestro on itself, in dozens of slightly different ways, to expand its range and scope utterly. List the
> improvements (small, medium, large: at least twenty-two) and the cross-pollinations (at least three dozen, with other
> parts of itself, with other skills' tools, protocols, harnesses and contexts, with online and offline sources), then
> execute them under three disciplines: code-cage (burn the obvious implementation), restriction-matrix (burn the two
> cheapest readings of every ask), fascination-forge (a detector must find a planted effect and stay quiet on nothing).
> Add the Maestro's opposite function. Consider what to take away, not only what to add. Check every skill at least
> briefly for what it could meld, and meld for general audiences. Brainstorm what I would want if I were the user and build
> from it. Reach pinnacles never reached before (measured), then smash them (measured again). Keep a receipt of what we
> have, can, will, can't and will not learn. Every claim measured, every feature with a failing-capable test, every
> number traceable to an output.

## B. My favorite prompt (P12: inspired by the user's best asks, this session and before)

> **Teach the tool to learn from what actually happened.** Take any tool we built that picks, ranks or recommends.
> Find the record of what was really used afterwards: it is free, it is honest, and it is already on disk. Measure the
> tool against that record blind, with no memory of its own ratings. Let the measurement choose what to add and what
> to take away. Leave a receipt a stranger could re-run. Then ask the tool what it cannot learn from that record, and
> write that down with the same care as the wins.

Why it is my favorite: it is the shape of the user's best prompts (speed-proof: "faster, same answers, with proof";
fairness: "no skill favoured for fame"; living-prompt: "write the prompt and run it"; this session: "what we can't and
will not learn") and it closes a loop with data nobody has to produce. v15's `hindsight` is this prompt, run once.
